A SMALL GUIDE TO BIG IDEAS
Meet your new workshop.
1. Choose & plot. Pick a primitive, press P, then click the canvas to add vertices. Press Enter or double-click to finish. Esc cancels. Backspace removes the last point. Quads use groups of four corners; Quad strip uses pairs across the strip. Circle takes two clicks: center, then radius.
2. Make it precise. Scroll to zoom around your cursor. The canvas runs from −1 to 1 on both axes. Zoom reveals finer decimal spacing without changing those bounds. Snap follows the current grid spacing. Hold Space and drag to pan. Use Grid in front to see the grid above filled shapes.
3. Shape & color. Press V to select. Drag a shape to move it, drag a vertex to reshape it, or enter exact X/Y values in the inspector. The color wheel edits the selection or your next object. For points, lines, strips, and loops, adjust Point size or Line width in the left panel (0.5–64 px). Select a polygon and press I to insert vertices on its existing edges; + in a vertex row inserts an exact midpoint. Drag the new point to reshape the same layer.
4. Stack your ideas. Layers at the top draw in front. Use the arrows or drag rows to reorder. Hide or lock each layer using its controls. Ctrl+D duplicates. Shift-click layers or drag a selection box to select several primitives. Ctrl+G creates a folder; Ctrl+Shift+G ungroups. Click a folder to select all its parts. Use Draw into to start another object on the same canvas.
5. Take it with you. Save a project to keep editable vertices. Export a simple GLUT/OpenGL .cpp program with reusable GLfloat vertex arrays, a JavaScript typed array, or JSON. Compile the .cpp to display your object in its own window. Hidden layers are omitted. Coordinates stay between −1 and 1. Build and run the active file using your course OpenGL workspace.
6. Transform together. Drag selected shapes to move them together. Press T for corner scale handles and the rotation handle above the selection. Hold Shift while rotating to snap to 15°. The inspector also accepts exact move, scale, and rotation values, plus Flip horizontal and Flip vertical. Flips use the selection’s shared center. Alt-click a grouped shape or select its child layer to edit just that primitive. Export content can be the entire canvas or just your selection.
